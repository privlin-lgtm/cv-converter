# CV norms research: US, UK, Germany, Israel, and Israeli army service

Researched 10 Oct 2026, ahead of the Developers Institute hackathon (15–20 Oct 2026). Five parallel passes: one per country and one on presenting Israeli military and national service abroad. Each pass cited only pages it opened.

**What this file is for.** On day 1 the tables below become the seed data for the `norms` table (see `intent/2026-10-10-mvp/spec.md`). Each table row is one seed row. The columns map like this:

| Table column | `norms` column | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| key | `key` | Unique per country. |
| rule | `rule` | Sent to the model as an instruction. |
| why | `why` | Shown to the user next to each change. |
| sources | `sources` (text[]) | Shown as links. Empty where none was found. |
| status | `status` | `confirmed` or `unverified`, from the first word of the research status (so "CONFIRMED (+972 format UNVERIFIED)" seeds as `confirmed`; the bracketed note stays here). Unverified rules are still used, but the UI labels them "common practice". |
| check | `check_def` (jsonb) | Only for the rules listed under "Suggested v1 checks" at the end; `null` for the rest. |

**Status legend.** CONFIRMED means an authoritative source states it, or two independent sources agree. UNVERIFIED means one weak source, or the researcher's inference.

**Wording rule for every country.** The legal rows (`legal_basis`, `legal_background`, `agg_sensitive_data`) are not rules for the CV. They tell the model how to phrase its explanations: removing age, marital status and so on is the *country's convention*, linked to the *employer's* anti-discrimination duties. It is never "illegal for you to include".

**Nuance (US)**
- **Length by level:** the sources only say "1–2 pages". The one-page-under-10-years split is common practice but unverified. Federal résumés have had a hard 2-page cap since 27 Sep 2025 (OPM).
- **Work authorization:** Drexel says leave visa status off; UNM says include it only if the employer asks. Treat it as the user's choice. The model asks; it never assumes.
- **Legal framing:** questions about age, marital status and similar topics are not illegal in themselves. EEOC calls them possible evidence of discriminatory intent. The ADEA covers only people aged 40+, at employers with 20+ employees.
- **GPA threshold:** UNM says 3.5+. Other guides often use 3.0 (unchecked).
- **Languages:** Israelis whose English isn't native may still want "English (fluent)". The level words are convention, not sourced.
- **Unsourced but standard:** tense, date style, paper size, US spelling, the header/footer warning, SSN.
- **Couldn't load:** EEOC age-inquiries page, CareerOneStop résumé guide, two Harvard PDFs, a USAJOBS help page.

**Nuance (UK)**
- **Legal scope:** ACAS tells employers to avoid asking for age or date of birth. Asking is not unlawful in itself, and no source says a candidate can't include these details. So removing them is convention, not law.
- **Nationality vs right to work:** the National Careers Service says leave nationality out; Oxford says include it only to show right to work. "Tier 2" is the old visa name and must not appear.
- **Address:** Prospects says full home address (except online CVs); Oxford says leave it out; Worcester says give your UK location. v1 uses town and country.
- **Profile:** Prospects and the National Careers Service recommend one; targetjobs leaves it out. v1 includes it.
- **File format:** Oxford says PDF; Prospects says check the advert.
- **Unsourced:** date-range format, British spelling and CEFR levels were inferred; searches only found résumé-builder sites. targetjobs is a commercial graduate-careers publisher.

**Nuance (Germany)**
- **Photo:** most sources say optional but common. H-BRS still calls it "erwünscht" (wanted). The federal anti-discrimination guide recommends employers anonymise applications. v1 never adds a photo and says it's the user's choice.
- **Personal data:** LSE calls a full date of birth "standard procedure"; the Bewerbungskompass says drop family details. v1 keeps date of birth only if supplied.
- **Signature:** every source opened still advises place, date and signature, including for electronic applications. No authoritative source calls it obsolete.
- **Chronology:** IHK Pfalz (writing for school-leavers) leans oldest first; all others say newest first.
- **Grades:** the formula is confirmed, but the Israeli lowest pass mark (Nmin) must come from anabin. Example: 88/100 with Nmin = 60 gives 1.9; Nmin = 60 is an assumption.
- **Source quality:** Uni-DUE is from 2009, the Köln guide from 2018; StepStone is a job board's magazine (signature row only).
- **Couldn't load:** two make-it-in-germany application pages, the AGG text on gesetze-im-internet.de (robots.txt), an Esslingen PDF.

**Hebrew CV notes (for a later version)**
- Right-to-left layout. Embedded English terms, emails, URLs and year ranges need bidi isolation, or ranges display reversed.
- Standard headings (MoD sample, Drushim): פרטים אישיים, תמצית, ניסיון תעסוקתי / ניסיון מקצועי, השכלה, שירות צבאי, קורסים, שפות, התנדבות, ממליצים – יינתנו לפי דרישה.
- Language lines follow the pattern "עברית – שפת אם", "אנגלית – טובה".
- Hebrew runs ~30% shorter than English (Telfed). Drushim advises translating by hand, not machine translation. NBN says Hebrew CVs get more callbacks.
- Hebrew verbs are gendered: use noun phrases (ניהול צוות) or ask the user's gender (inference).

**Nuance (Israel)**
- **Military placement:** the MoD template for discharged soldiers puts it right after the summary; Drushim 13 puts it near the end, "if relevant". Default for olim: near the end.
- **Summary length:** Drushim says 1–2 sentences; Telfed says 3–5. v1 uses 2–4.
- **Photo:** Drushim 118 (as extracted) mentions a photo on English CVs while noting it differs from Israeli practice; Telfed says no photo. v1 removes it.
- **Personal details:** the 2016 MoD template still shows ID number and date of birth (older or public-sector practice). Current guides drop them.
- **Degree evaluation:** per gov.il, olim arriving from 1 Jan 2025 go to the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration's division; earlier arrivals stay with the Ministry of Education. Its stated uses are public-sector.
- **Agencies wanting Word:** not confirmed. Drushim says Word or an unlocked PDF; NBN says Word or PDF.
- **Law:** Kol Zchut says the list of grounds is open-ended and the law fully applies at 6+ employees. It governs employers, not CV content.
- **Not reached:** Gvahim, taasuka.gov.il, AllJobs, JobMaster, a university career-centre guide. English Kol Zchut was removed in 2023.

**Nuance (army service)**
- **Omit or include:** Drushim says service "can be omitted or included depending on the hiring company" for foreign firms. Germany's gap-free norm makes leaving it out risky there. For senior US/UK candidates, one line is the safe middle ground. No UK-specific source was found.

- **Germany:** the researcher's unsourced inference is that a neutral, factual tone (function, skills, dates; no combat emphasis) is lower-risk.

## Suggested v1 checks (deterministic, run on the model's output)

The `check_def` column holds JSON with one of four types. Only these rules get a check in v1; the rest stay `null`.
- `absent`: a regex that must not match the plain-text CV.
- `present`: a regex that must match.
- `max_words`: a word budget.
- `all_dates_match`: applied to each entry's `dates` value. The value is split on "–" or "-", and each part must match the pattern or be an allowed word ("Present", or "since MM/YYYY").
